I'm a backend engineer. Right now I work on infrastructure for quantitative
research and crypto analytics — the pipelines, services and data plumbing that
sit underneath other people's decisions. Before that I was in consumer fintech,
building backend services at [MoneyLion](https://www.moneylion.com/about/).

I studied Computer Science at Monash University Malaysia, and picked up the
data-engineering and distributed-systems side of it along the way. Most of what
I actually use day to day I learned by building the smallest working version of
something and then breaking it.

## The finance part

The short version is that I find markets more interesting than almost anything
else I could be reading about.

It started as curiosity about where money actually goes and turned into a proper
habit: reading annual reports, working through how a business really makes money,
and occasionally writing up what I find — like the
[Ping An analysis](/posts/ping-an-analysis), which took considerably longer than
I expected.

Crypto pulled me in from the engineering side rather than the price side. I
wanted to know how a chain really works, so I
[wrote one](https://github.com/GeorgeTan615/Simple-Blockchain) — blocks, hashing,
proof-of-work, [UTXOs](/posts/unspent-transaction-outputs). That turned out to be
a much better teacher than any explainer I'd read.

Most of my side projects live at that intersection. A
[signal bot](https://github.com/GeorgeTan615/SignalB) that evaluates historical
price data against strategies I define. A
[tracker](https://github.com/GeorgeTan615/FinvTracker) for investment performance,
because the picture was split across five apps and a spreadsheet.

## Why I write

A friend from an old job kept a daily blog and pushed me to start one. He was
right. The thing I keep coming back to:

> You learn best when you explain it to others — and if you can't explain it well
> enough to others, you don't understand it well enough yet.

So the writing here is mostly me checking my own work. Some of it is markets,
some is engineering, and some of it is just a
[rough day](/posts/rough-day-at-work) I felt like writing down.
